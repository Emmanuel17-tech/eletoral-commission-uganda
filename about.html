01 / The institution

Understanding the Commission begins with its place in Uganda's constitutional and electoral framework.

An electoral institution with a public responsibility.

The Electoral Commission of Uganda is established under Article 60 of the Constitution of the Republic of Uganda, 1995. Its constitutional role centres on organising, conducting and supervising regular, free and fair elections and referenda.

Its work extends beyond polling day. It includes voter registration, electoral boundary demarcation, voter education and the handling of election complaints within its legal mandate.

These responsibilities connect election administration with citizens' ability to participate in the electoral process and understand how it works.

02 / Constitutional mandate

What the Commission is mandated to do.

Article 61 sets out the Commission's core constitutional functions. Together, these responsibilities cover the electoral process from voter registration to results.

61

Organising elections and referenda under the Constitution.

Constitutional reference: Article 61 of the Constitution of the Republic of Uganda, 1995.

  1. 01

    Ensure that regular, free and fair elections and referenda are held.

  2. 02

    Organise, conduct and supervise elections and referenda in accordance with the Constitution.

  3. 03

    Demarcate constituencies in accordance with constitutional provisions.

  4. 04

    Compile, maintain, revise and update the voters' register.

  5. 05

    Formulate and implement voter education programmes relating to elections.

  6. 06

    Hear and determine election complaints arising before and during polling.

  7. 07

    Ascertain, publish and declare election and referendum results in accordance with the law.

This is a summary for the design concept, not a substitute for the complete constitutional text or applicable electoral laws.

03 / Independence & accountability

Independence is part of the constitutional design.

The Constitution addresses not only the Commission's responsibilities but also its independence. Article 62 provides that, in performing its constitutional functions, the Commission shall be free from the direction or control of any person or authority.

This constitutional provision forms part of the framework governing how the Commission carries out its electoral responsibilities.

Constitutional reference · Article 62

Independence in the performance of the Commission's functions is expressly addressed by the Constitution. This summary should be read alongside the full legal text.

04 / How the process works

An election is a process, not just a day.

Electoral activities take place in stages. The exact schedule depends on the type of election and the applicable official programme.

01

Voter registration

The voters' register is compiled and maintained in accordance with the applicable electoral framework.

02

Register updates

Voter register updates and display activities take place according to official arrangements and deadlines.

03

Voter education

Citizens receive information intended to help them understand electoral procedures and participation.

04

Candidate nominations

Candidates participate in nomination procedures governed by applicable laws and official schedules.

05

Campaign period

Campaign activities take place within the applicable legal framework and announced timetable.

06

Polling day

Eligible voters cast their ballots according to the procedures established for the election.

07

Results declaration

Results are ascertained, published and declared in accordance with applicable law.

08

Electoral complaints

Election complaints are handled through the relevant procedures and within the Commission's legal mandate.

This is an explanatory overview, not a live election calendar. Always consult official notices for dates, eligibility rules and procedural requirements.

05 / Guiding principles

The principles that give the process its meaning.

Electoral administration depends on a clear legal framework, professional practice and information that people can access and understand.

01

Impartiality

Electoral responsibilities must be carried out within the applicable legal framework and without improper direction or control.

02

Professionalism

Election administration requires organised procedures, trained personnel and careful management of electoral work.

03

Public information

Accessible voter education and clear official notices help citizens understand procedures and deadlines.

04

Accountability

Legal responsibilities, established procedures and official records provide a framework for public scrutiny.
